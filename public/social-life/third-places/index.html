Third places that aren’t bars: where to go after work

The sociologist Ray Oldenburg called home your first place and work your second. A third place is where you choose to spend time around other people, and keep going back to. For a lot of adults the only obvious one is a bar, which gets expensive and isn’t for everyone.

Why it’s worth finding one

In 2023 the US Surgeon General published an advisory on loneliness. It compares the mortality risk of being socially disconnected to smoking up to 15 cigarettes a day. It also found that people aged 15 to 24 spent about 40 minutes a day with friends in person in 2020, down from about 150 minutes in 2003.

What makes a place a third place

You can turn up without a plan. You see some of the same people each time. You can stay without having to keep buying things. A place that does all three is worth more than a nicer one you visit once.

Free or close to it

  • Libraries. Many run talks, language exchanges, craft nights and game nights.
  • Silent reading meetups in a park. In Bengaluru, Cubbon Reads meets in Cubbon Park every Saturday morning: bring a book and a mat, and read alongside everyone else.
  • Parks, at the same hour each week. Pickup games, chess tables and dog runs all have regulars.
  • Running and walking clubs. The conversation ends on its own when the run does, which makes the first one easier.
  • Community gardens. You come back for the plants and end up knowing the people.
  • A regular volunteering shift. The same evening each week puts you next to the same people.

Worth a small spend

  • Climbing gyms. People talk between climbs, and regulars notice new faces.
  • Ceramics studios with open studio hours.
  • Bookshops and cafés that host readings or swaps.
  • Chess and board game clubs. Some new ones are drawing big crowds of people in their twenties.
  • Social sports leagues. The drink afterward is optional.

How to become a regular

  1. Choose one place and plan four visits before you decide whether it’s for you. The first visit is almost always the awkward one.
  2. Go at the same time each week, so you keep running into the same people.
  3. Bring a friend once, on the visit you’d otherwise talk yourself out of.
  4. Pick places with something to do. Talking is easier when you’re both looking at the same wall or board.

How Soosh helps

On Soosh, people run Spaces for the things they love and post events you can go to. When a host confirms you came, you get a Was there Stub. Log Moments with a photo in one Corner, like climbing or chess, on 10 different days in a month and you get a Regular Stub.
