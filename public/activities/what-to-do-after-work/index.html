What to do after work when you’re tired, short on money, or on your own

A lot of evenings end on the couch for one reason: the deciding happens after you’ve already sat down. These ideas are sorted by what you actually have at the end of the day: energy, money, and people.

Decide on the way home

Pick tonight’s plan before you close your laptop or on your commute, then go straight there. If you go home first, the plan has to beat the couch, and the couch usually wins.

Tired, no budget, on your own

  • Get off the train a stop early, or walk home a way you’ve never taken.
  • Go to the library and borrow one book from a shelf you never look at.
  • Cook something you haven’t made before, using only what’s in your kitchen.
  • Many museums have a free or pay what you wish evening. Look up the ones near you and go for 45 minutes.
  • Stretch along to a 20 minute video, then go to bed early.

Tired, with a friend

  • Walk and talk instead of meeting for a drink.
  • Cook dinner together and split the groceries.
  • Play one short board game at someone’s place.
  • Watch a film at home and argue about the ending.

Some energy, small budget

  • A drop-in class at a community center: pottery, dance, life drawing.
  • Pub trivia. You need a team name more than you need answers.
  • A running club. Many are free, and nobody expects you to be fast on your first night.
  • An open mic. Go as audience the first time.
  • A thrift store challenge with a friend: same budget each, best find wins.

Full energy, happy to spend

  • Bouldering. Gyms rent shoes, and many run a short intro for first-timers.
  • A one-night workshop: bread, printmaking, knife skills.
  • A season in a social sports league.
  • A cooking class for two, then make the dish again at home the next week.

When you have nothing left

Some nights you should rest. Make it a choice: phone in another room, a long shower, one album start to finish, a chapter of a book.

Do it twice

Pick one thing from this page and do it twice in the next two weeks. Bring someone the second time. Going back is how an idea becomes part of your week.

How Soosh helps

When you join Soosh, you tell it which evenings you’re usually free, roughly what you want to spend in a month, and any passes you already have, like a climbing gym or a library card. Home then suggests things to do this week that fit. It leans toward free things if your budget is zero, and it can show you when a friend is already going. Soosh is for people 18 and over.
