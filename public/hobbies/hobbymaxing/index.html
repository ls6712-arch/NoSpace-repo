Hobbymaxing: how to keep more than one hobby going

Hobbymaxing is our word for keeping a few hobbies going at once and getting properly better at them, often with friends. The aim is three you’re still doing next year, rather than ten you bought gear for.

Why hobbies stall early

The first session runs on novelty. By the second or third, you can see the gap between what you made and what you pictured, and nothing in particular pulls you back. People who keep going usually have something holding them in place: a set time, a person who expects them, or a way to see their progress.

Pick a stack

A good stack has a few hobbies that do different jobs:

  • One that moves you. Climbing, running, dance, swimming.
  • One that leaves something behind. Ceramics, cooking, sewing, photography, woodwork.
  • One where you learn or play with others. A language, chess, a book club, an instrument.

They feed each other. A run clears your head before you write. A pottery class gives you something to photograph. A language gives you recipes to try. When one stalls for a few weeks, the others keep your evenings full.

Fit it into a normal week

  1. Give each hobby its own evening. Tuesday is climbing, Thursday is clay. A set slot means one less decision each day.
  2. Leave one evening open for whatever friends suggest.
  3. Keep the minimum small. On a bad week, 20 minutes of sketching still counts as showing up.
  4. Let the stack change with the seasons. Outdoor hobbies can take the summer and indoor ones the winter.

Getting past the first few sessions

  • Set a small finish line, like one mug you’d actually drink from, or a 5K without stopping. Open-ended practice is hard to stick with.
  • Do it with someone. A friend who’s coming is the best reason to go on a tired night.
  • Take a photo of every attempt. When it doesn’t feel like you’re improving, the photos show you are.

Mistakes to skip

Don’t buy the full kit before your third session. Rent or borrow until you know you’ll keep going. Don’t start three new hobbies in the same month. Add one once the last one feels routine. And when a hobby stops being fun, drop it and give that evening to one of the others.

How Soosh helps

A Pursuit on Soosh is something you’re working toward, like that first mug or that 5K. You can set a goal, log Moments as you go, and invite friends to do it with you. When you finish, you get a Finisher Stub on your Shelf. Log Moments with a photo in the same Corner on 10 different days in a month and you get a Regular Stub too.
