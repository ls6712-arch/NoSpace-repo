How to get your money’s worth from memberships you already pay for

Add up what you pay each month for a gym, a class pack, a museum membership, maybe a perk through work. Then count the evenings those turned into last month. For most people there’s a gap, and you can close a lot of it without buying anything new.

1. List what you have

Go through your bank statement and write down every membership and subscription. Then add the ones that cost nothing and are easy to forget: your library card, alumni access, benefits through work, and perks on your credit card or phone plan.

2. Find the extras

  • Gyms often include guest passes, group classes or a pool that members never try.
  • Library cards can cover a lot more than books. In New York, cardholders of the New York, Brooklyn and Queens public libraries can reserve free passes to museums, theaters and gardens through Culture Pass. Passes are limited, and new ones are released on the first of each month for the month after.
  • Museum memberships usually come with member hours, talks and guest tickets.
  • Work benefits sometimes include a fitness or learning budget. Look in your benefits portal. These are easy to miss.
  • Alumni groups often run events and clubs in bigger cities.

3. Give each one an evening

A membership you use whenever you feel like it gets used rarely. Give each one a regular slot: Monday is the spin class, Wednesday is the library’s film night, the first Friday of the month is the museum’s late opening.

4. Use guest passes on purpose

Guest passes are easy to forget, and they’re the best perk for your social life. Invite the friend who keeps saying you should do something. If they have a membership you don’t, trade: your gym’s pool for their museum’s late night.

5. Cancel what still sits unused

After a month of trying, cancel whatever you still haven’t touched. Put that money toward one thing you’d look forward to, like a workshop or a season in a sports league.
